Introduction

This Privacy Policy applies to OpenNote: ScribblePad ("OpenNote"), including the Android app with package name com.kararnab.bestnote, available on Google Play. OpenNote is a multiplatform note-taking application available on Android, iOS, Desktop, and Web.

OpenNote is developed and published by ARNAB KAR, an individual developer based in India, under the Google Play developer name A Storyteller. ARNAB KAR is responsible for the app and for this Privacy Policy. In this policy, "we" and "us" refer to ARNAB KAR. This policy is hosted on the developer's website, 1loom.com.

Your privacy is important to us. This Privacy Policy explains how your data is handled when you use OpenNote.

Data Collection

OpenNote's note-taking features work offline, and note content is stored locally on your device. Optional usage analytics and crash reporting are available only in builds configured for PostHog, are off by default, and have separate controls in Settings. When both options are off, OpenNote does not send analytics or crash reports.

Your note content is stored locally on your device. The developer has no access to your notes.

If you enable usage analytics, OpenNote sends a random app-specific identifier, a limited event name (app opened on native platforms; page view on the Web; note created; ink added, at most once per app launch; search used; note shared as a PDF or image; or backup saved or restored), and the app platform and version. The Web page-view event does not include the page URL. OpenNote does not send note content, titles, tags, search terms, drawings, images, or note identifiers. PostHog receives connection data such as your IP address when an event is sent. You can turn analytics off in Settings at any time; this stops future events but does not remove events already sent.

If you enable crash reporting, an unhandled exception is saved locally and submitted to PostHog on the next app launch. A report includes a random app-specific identifier, the exception type and message, and its stack trace, along with the app platform and version. OpenNote does not attach note content, but exception messages or stack data supplied by the operating system, browser, or libraries may contain information. PostHog receives connection data such as your IP address when a report is sent. Turning crash reporting off stops future reports and clears any report still waiting on the device; it does not remove reports already sent.

Selecting Feedback opens a PostHog-hosted survey. Any response you choose to submit is sent directly to PostHog. The survey link does not include a respondent identifier, so responses are anonymous by default. Do not include note content or other information you do not want to share. PostHog may receive connection data such as your IP address when you open or submit the survey.

On Android, the app's data (your notes and app settings) may be included in Android's standard device backup to your Google account, if you have turned on backup in your device settings. This backup is provided and controlled by Google and your device, not by the developer, and the developer cannot access it.

When you back up your notes from Settings, OpenNote creates a backup file on your device containing all your notes, folders, tags and the pictures on your pages, including notes in the bin. The file is not encrypted. You choose where it goes, through your device's share sheet or save dialog; if you save it to a cloud service or send it to another app, that service's privacy policy applies. Restoring reads a backup file you choose. The developer never receives your backup files.

Pictures you add to a page come from your device's photo picker or camera app and are stored with your notes on your device. Small JPEG and PNG pictures are kept as the original file, so they may still contain metadata the camera added, such as the date or location, and that metadata is included in backup files.

If you choose to enable optional features in the future (such as cloud backup, synchronization, or account-based services), we will collect only the data necessary to provide those features, such as your email address and note content.

How Your Data Is Used

Your notes remain local. If enabled, limited usage events and crash details are sent to PostHog to help understand app use and diagnose reliability issues. Feedback you submit through the hosted survey is used to improve OpenNote. PostHog's privacy policy is available at posthog.com/privacy.

When you choose to share or export a note as a PDF, or a page as an image, the file is created on your device and passed to the app you select. That app's own privacy policy then applies. Links in the app (such as to the developer's website or the Google Play page) open in your browser or the Play Store.

If optional online features such as sync or cloud backup are introduced and enabled, your data will be used only for:

  • Syncing notes across your devices
  • Providing cloud backup and restore
  • Maintaining app performance and reliability

We do not sell or rent your personal data.

Ads & Monetization

OpenNote currently contains no advertisements and no advertising SDKs. In the future, the free version of OpenNote may include advertisements. If ads are added, this policy will be updated to name the advertising providers before that version is released.

Advertising providers may collect limited information such as device identifiers, IP address, and app usage data to display relevant ads, in accordance with their own privacy policies.

The premium version of OpenNote will remain ad-free.

Data Storage & Security

Notes and any crash report waiting to be sent are stored locally on your device. When an enabled PostHog feature sends an event or report, PostHog processes the information described above under its own privacy practices. Usage events, crash reports, and feedback submitted through the hosted survey are retained in the configured PostHog project.

If cloud features are introduced, we will implement security measures such as encryption in transit (HTTPS) and secure storage practices.

Permissions

The Android app declares Internet access so that enabled, PostHog-configured analytics or crash reporting can send data. These features make no PostHog requests unless you enable their separate Settings controls.

The Android app does not request access to your storage, photos or camera. Adding a picture uses the system photo picker, which shares only the picture you select; taking a photo opens your device's camera app; and saving or opening backup and export files uses the system share sheet or file dialogs. On iPhone and iPad, iOS asks for camera access the first time you take a photo in OpenNote. Any permission a future feature needs will be used only for that feature and described here.

Third-Party Services

When configured for a build and enabled by you in Settings, OpenNote uses PostHog for limited product analytics and crash reporting. These settings are separate and off by default. The Feedback survey is hosted by PostHog and opens only when you select Feedback. No advertising SDK is currently included. See PostHog's privacy policy for information about its handling of submitted data. Future third-party services for syncing, backups, or ads will be described in this policy before they are introduced.

Children’s Privacy

OpenNote is intended for general audiences and does not knowingly collect personal data from children.

If we become aware that personal data from a child has been collected without parental consent, we will delete it.

Data Retention and Deletion

Your notes stay on your device until you delete them in the app (notes moved to the bin can be deleted permanently), clear the app's data, or uninstall the app. Because the developer does not receive your notes, there is nothing for the developer to retain or delete on your behalf. Backup and export files you saved stay wherever you saved them until you delete them. Turning off analytics or crash reporting stops future submissions; it does not delete data already received by PostHog.

Your Rights

Depending on your location, you may have rights to access, update, or delete your data.

If account-based features are introduced, you will be able to request deletion of your data.

Changes to This Policy

We may update this Privacy Policy from time to time. Continued use of OpenNote after changes indicates acceptance of the updated policy.
